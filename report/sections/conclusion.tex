\section{Conclusion}
Pairwise net influence detects an instructed LLM cartel in a repeated
procurement auction, but a simple correlation of the same data detects it
as well or better. Its distinct value appears when one firm's private cost
flows into another firm's bids: for espionage it beat correlation, and this
held on fresh markets. The most profitable collusion, a cartel without a
channel, was the hardest to detect, and a small LLM judge given ample tokens
judged firm strength rather than coordination. Behaviour-based screens can
support an investigation; they do not establish agreement, intent or harm.
