\section{Conclusion}
Collusion, espionage, or look-alikes? In this auction the audit could tell
them apart, but only partly from single markets. From the bids of AI agents
alone, a calibrated net-influence alarm found cartels that chatted and
named the right pair without wrong accusations. With private costs, pooled
evidence showed which way information flowed, two-way for the cartel and
one-way for the spy, but single-market alarms almost never caught the spy,
which barely used what it stole. A simple correlation detected chatting
cartels as well, the most profitable cartel kept a fixed price and stayed
hidden, and a small AI judge looked at firm strength. The alarm needs only
public bids, but common cost shocks and fewer tenders would make real
markets harder. Such screens can direct an investigation but do not
prove agreement, intent or harm.
