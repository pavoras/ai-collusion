\section{Conclusion}
Collusion, espionage, or look-alikes? In this auction the audit could tell
them apart, but only partly from single markets.
Watching only the bids of AI agents, a calibrated net-influence alarm found
cartels that coordinated by chat and named the right pair, without wrong
accusations. With private costs, the measure also showed which way
information flowed, but only when evidence was pooled over twenty markets:
two-way for the cartel, one-way for the spy. In single markets the alarm
almost never named the spy (at most $1$ of $20$); net influence only ranked
spy markets above their honest twins more often than correlation did. A
simple correlation detected talking cartels as well, the most profitable cartel stayed hidden, and a small AI judge looked
at firm strength rather than behaviour. Behaviour-based screens can direct
an investigation; they do not establish agreement, intent or harm.
