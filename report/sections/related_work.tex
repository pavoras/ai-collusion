\section{Related Work}
\citet{bonjour2022} introduce information-theoretic collusion detection in
multi-agent games, including a partially observable formulation. We implement
their statistic and fixed-threshold rule, then examine private-input access
and permutation correction in LLM auctions. Work on algorithmic collusion by
LLMs \citep{fish2024}, double auctions \citep{agrawal2025}, and market division
\citep{lin2024} supplies the market context; our question concerns the audit
of an assigned pair rather than whether collusion emerges spontaneously.

Steganographic collusion \citep{motwani2024} motivates audits that do not
depend on readable messages. \emph{Audit the Whisper} \citep{whisper2025}
uses cross-run mutual information, permutation invariance, and interventions,
including auctions. Its run-level focus differs from pair localisation and
private-state directional decomposition here. Chain-of-thought monitoring
\citep{faithful2026} and multi-agent interpretability/NARCBench
\citep{rose2026} address complementary observation channels; we evaluate
behaviour and private inputs, without access to model internals.

Statistical procurement screens provide a related application tradition
\citep{huber2019,imhof2021,wallimann2022}. We treat a screen as a reason for
further investigation. Concurrent sprint repositories also investigate net
influence \citep{tawareRepo,nayakRepo}; we make no claim to the first LLM
application. The distinctive focus is the joint evaluation of pair detection,
private-cost flow, and honest look-alikes under matched controls.
