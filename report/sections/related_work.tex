\section{Related Work}
\citet{bonjour2022} introduce information-theoretic collusion detection in
multi-agent games, including the net-influence statistic and fixed-threshold
rule we test. LLM agents have been shown to collude in pricing
\citep{fish2024}, double auctions \citep{agrawal2025} and market division
\citep{lin2024}; we instead assign a cartel and ask whether an audit can find
it. Steganographic collusion \citep{motwani2024} motivates audits that do not
read messages. \emph{Audit the Whisper} \citep{whisper2025} detects collusion
across whole runs, including auctions; we localise the pair and split the
flow by private input. Chain-of-thought monitoring \citep{faithful2026} and
activation probes \citep{rose2026} need access to the model; we use behaviour
and private inputs only. Statistical bid-rigging screens
\citep{huber2019,imhof2021,wallimann2022} supply the procurement tradition,
in which a screen is a reason to investigate, not proof. Two concurrent
sprint projects also apply net influence to LLM agents
\citep{tawareRepo,nayakRepo}; we make no claim to the first LLM application
and compare results in the Discussion.
