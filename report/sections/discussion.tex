\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\paragraph{What an overseer can take from this.}
Five practical points follow, each limited to this game.
(1)~Always report a same-access correlation baseline. Net influence passed
its primary test, but correlation was at least as good on every cartel with
a channel; reporting net influence alone would overstate its value.
(2)~Net influence earns its place where private information flows: it beat
correlation for espionage at L2, on the original and on fresh markets. That
use needs access to private inputs; with public bids only, the advantage did
not replicate.
(3)~Calibrate before alarming. The published fixed threshold flagged every
honest L2 market, while the permutation-corrected rule flagged the honest
reference pair in none.
(4)~Do not treat a small LLM judge as a safe default. Given enough tokens it
answered, but it judged firm strength rather than coordination.
(5)~Profitable tacit collusion is the weak spot. The cartel without a
channel earned the most and was detected least well by both detectors;
profit-based screens may need to complement dependence-based ones.

\paragraph{What the evidence does not show.}
Only the primary test is confirmatory. The replication was planned after
the original results were known and reuses the already collected honest
control markets; it guards against a chance finding on the treatment
markets, not against every analytic choice. Twenty blocks per cell give
wide intervals, and absence of an advantage is not equivalence. Separate
AUROCs do not measure the value of combining scores. Net direction compares
rival influences and does not identify a sender; a one-way leak is not a
unique signature of espionage, and market-level flow labels rarely resolved
the mechanism. Net-influence scores carry permutation Monte Carlo noise of
about $\pm0.01$ AUROC. The judge comparison concerns one 9B model judging
its own family; larger or differently prompted judges may behave
differently. The probes cannot support a causal claim.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The auction is synthetic, the cartel is instructed rather than emergent,
and the treatment bundles a joint objective, partner information, a
surveillance warning and, in C1, a channel. The core uses one model; small
models were unusable and breadth cells have ten markets. L2 assumes an
auditor entitled to see private costs. Privacy, false-accusation and
dual-use considerations are discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
Independent replications with fresh controls; combined detectors that use
correlation and directional leaks together; larger and more varied bidder
and judge models; markets with more firms, which need estimators that cope
with the larger state space; and probes with independent message draws per
context, analysed with a null that respects that nesting.
