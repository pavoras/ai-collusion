\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\paragraph{Expected versus observed.}
Before collecting any LLM data we simulated scripted bidders that always
follow their strategy, with the same game, rules and sample sizes
(Appendix~\ref{app:scripted}). Figure~\ref{fig:expectations} compares that
plan with what the AI agents did. The calibration carried over: honest
markets produced false alarms at about the simulated rate. The power did
not. Scripted cartels were named in about $99\%$ of markets and scripted
spies in $100\%$; the AI cartels in $65$--$100\%$ with bids only, and the
AI spy in none. Even scripted spies that used the stolen cost only half the
time were found in $83\%$. Flow direction shows the same gap: two-way flow
was expected in every strong-pair cartel market and one-way flow in $80\%$
of spy markets, but was seen in $0\%$ and $20\%$; only pooling over markets
recovered it (Figure~\ref{fig:flow}). AI agents appear to act on shared
information far less consistently than scripts, so their traces are weaker
than a simulation with rule-following agents suggests.

\paragraph{Comparison with the original paper and concurrent work.}
Our scripted replication of \citet{bonjour2022} recovered their rising
detection pattern in rock--paper--scissors, but not every published
percentage (Appendix~\ref{app:replication}). Two concurrent sprint projects
studied the same measure in rock--paper--scissors. \citet{tawareRepo} also
found the fixed threshold unreliable (an innocent pair accused in $29\%$ of
100-round games) and a permutation-calibrated test near $2\%$ false alarms,
matching our alarm results. They report, however, that two Qwen agents with
near-identical prompts were flagged in $7$ of $10$ games without colluding;
in our auction, honest markets of four Qwen agents raised one false alarm
in $40$, and swapping same-model placements had no detectable effect. The
auction's conditioning on costs and the last winning price may explain the
difference; we did not test this. \citet{nayakRepo} found that net
influence and Cram\'er's V named the planted pair equally often with
scripted agents, as our correlation results do for talking cartels; we add
that the exception is private information flow seen with costs.
\citet{rose2026} detect collusion from model activations with AUROC $0.99$
to $1.00$ and report that only $2$ of $229$ collusive episodes in one
environment verbalised the coordination. Behaviour-only audits like ours
need no model access but, as the quiet cartel shows, miss coordination that
leaves no trace in actions; the two approaches complement each other.

\paragraph{What this means in practice.}
Each point is limited to this game.
(1)~\emph{Screening from public bids is feasible.} An overseer who sees
only bids could have used a calibrated alarm to point to the right pair in
most markets where AI agents coordinated actively, with few false alarms
and no wrong accusations.
(2)~\emph{Calibrate before alarming.} The fixed threshold published with
the method flagged most or all honest markets; a permutation correction
fixed this for the main model, but not fully for Ministral (6 of 20), so
calibration has to be checked per model.
(3)~\emph{Use costs to understand, not to detect.} Costs did not improve
detection of talking cartels, but pooled over many markets they show which
way information flows and separate a two-way cartel from a one-way spy.
(4)~\emph{Always compare with a simple baseline.} A plain correlation did
as well for talking cartels; net influence earned its place only for
espionage.
(5)~\emph{Watch profits too, and do not trust a small AI judge.} The cartel
without chat earned the most and was nearly invisible to both measures. The
9B judge judged firm strength rather than coordination.
(6)~\emph{Do not plan with rule-following simulations alone.} They
predicted false alarms well but overstated detection power for AI agents.

\paragraph{What the evidence does not show.}
Only the primary test is confirmatory; alarm counts, flow patterns and the
simulation comparison are secondary. The replication was planned after the
original results were known and reuses the honest control markets. Twenty
markets per type give wide intervals, and absence of an advantage is not
equivalence. A one-way leak is not a unique signature of espionage, and the
pooled flow intervals are approximate. The judge result concerns one 9B
model. Comparisons with concurrent work cross games and implementations.
The probes cannot support a causal claim.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The auction is synthetic, the cartel is instructed rather than emergent,
and the treatment bundles a joint objective, partner information, a
surveillance warning and, in C1, a chat channel. The core uses one model;
small models were unusable and breadth cells have only 20 markets. Privacy,
false-accusation and dual-use considerations are discussed in
Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}. Next steps: independent replications; screens
that combine correlation, flow direction and profits; larger bidder and
judge models; and markets with more firms.
