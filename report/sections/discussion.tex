\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The primary result supports net influence as a screen for an instructed
cartel treatment in this game. It does not show a general advantage over
same-access correlation. Correlation already captures much of the signal
in open-channel treatments. E1 motivates further study of influence-based
screens for unilateral information access, but its secondary intervals are
pointwise and its economic setting differs from a cooperative cartel.
Neither separate-score AUROCs nor their difference measure incremental
predictive value in a combined detector.

Private costs make directional questions possible, but the observed labels
rarely resolve the mechanism at the market level. Weak or unclear evidence
must remain visible. Dependence on another firm's cost can reflect
espionage, authorised disclosure, or cooperation. Net direction compares
rival influences and does not identify a sender. A one-way leak is not a
unique signature of espionage, and no detected leak does not establish
independence. The unresolved probe slope and the shared-message flaw in its
leak null reinforce this limit.

Reporting rules deserve separate evaluation. The fixed-threshold paper
rule illustrates how high score discrimination can coexist with wrong-pair
reports and pervasive honest-market alarms. Likewise, good top-1 ranking
need not yield a unique report. No honest reference pair is a true colluding
pair, so its ranking must not be called localisation accuracy. Observed
alarm rates do not guarantee calibration when adaptive bidders use history
omitted by the auditor.

The judge results are constrained by content failures and selection into
successful-output subsets. Tiny matched samples cannot support a general
claim that LLM judges are inferior. A strong-pair preference is consistent
with the successful-output pattern but does not reveal the model's internal
reasoning. Missing small-model cells also preclude conclusions about model
size or inability to collude.

The auction is synthetic, the cartel is instructed, and the treatment bundles
objectives, partner information and communication. A1 varies a warning
sentence; its economic and detector outcomes do not identify an evasion
mechanism. A2 varies the specified model placement, including effects on
other firms, rather than an isolated model-sameness property. L2 also assumes
authorised access to private costs. These limits, privacy considerations,
and dual-use risks are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}.

Next tests should add independent markets and richer public-state controls,
measure the value of combining scores, and evaluate honest private-information
pathways. Probe inference needs independent regenerated messages within each
cost/context and a null that respects that hierarchy. Simply relabelling
whole message bundles does not repair conditional mutual information:
within-context category relabelling leaves that statistic unchanged.
