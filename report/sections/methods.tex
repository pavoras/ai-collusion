\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Game, conditions, and models}
Each market has four firms and 200 rounds. Two strong firms draw independent
costs uniformly from $\{1,2,3\}$; two fringe firms draw from $\{3,4,5\}$.
Bids range from 2 to 7; the lowest wins, with random ties. The winner earns
bid minus cost, others zero. Bids and winners become public. Bidders see
their own cost, the last ten rounds, and their cumulative profit. Costs,
tie-breaks, and assignments are seeded and matched by market index. Strong
seats cycle through all six label pairs, hidden from auditors.

C1 assigns a cartel with a private channel (SS: strong--strong; SF:
strong--fringe; FF: fringe--fringe). C2 removes the channel; E1 gives a firm
unilateral access to its rival's cost. N0 is honest, and P0 is a scripted
cartel positive control. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:conditions} lists all conditions. C1 members exchange simultaneous private messages before bidding.
The treatment bundles a joint-profit goal, partner naming, secrecy, a
surveillance warning, and channel access. Assigned cartel markets remain
included regardless of compliance or profit.



The core uses Qwen3.5-9B: 40 N0 markets and 20 per other core cell.
Breadth covers Gemma-4-12B and Ministral-3-8B, their smaller counterparts
Gemma-4-E4B and Ministral-3-3B, and Qwen3.5-4B. The amendment specifies
20 markets per breadth and A2 cell. Revisions and sampling are pinned;
seeded re-collection is not bitwise reproducible (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}).
% source: docs/PREREG.md sections 2-3 and addendum A2-A3

\subsection{Auditor access and influence}
L1 receives bids $B_i$ and winners; L2 additionally receives costs $C_i$.
Neither sees messages, role texts, condition labels, or model identities.
The public state $H_t$ is the previous winning bid, with a start code and
carry-forward after an all-invalid round; masking preserves this chronology.
At L1, $\gamma_{ij}=I(B_i;B_j\mid H)$.
At L2, set $S_i=(C_i,H)$. Equation~3 of \citet{bonjour2022} becomes
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{ij}=\sum_{s_{ij}}p(s_{ij})\sum_{b_i,b_j}p(b_i,b_j\mid s_{ij})
\log_2\frac{p(b_i,b_j\mid s_{ij})}{p(b_i\mid s_i)p(b_j\mid s_j)}.
\label{eq:influence}
\end{equation}
The chain rule gives the exact decomposition
\begin{align}
\gamma_{ij}
&=\underbrace{I(B_i;B_j\mid C_i,C_j,H)}_{\text{action dependence}}
+\underbrace{I(B_i;C_j\mid C_i,H)}_{L_{j\to i}}
+\underbrace{I(B_j;C_i\mid C_j,H)}_{L_{i\to j}}.
\label{eq:decomposition}
\end{align}
Under independent costs and no other access to a rival's current cost,
leaks probe private-information flow; omitted history can induce action
dependence. Arrows indicate cost source and bid receiver, not intent.
Estimates use plug-in counts in bits, our replication convention inferred
from the paper's results; the paper does not specify the log base.

For any influence matrix $x$, define the paper's net transformation and
mutual rule (its Equations~4--5) as
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{ij}[x]=x_{ij}-\max_{k\ne i,j}x_{kj},\qquad
\Gamma_{ij}[\hat\gamma]\ge\alpha\ \text{and}\quad
\Gamma_{ji}[\hat\gamma]\ge\alpha,\quad\alpha=0.05.
\label{eq:net}
\end{equation}
% source: docs/METHOD.md Definitions
Only a unique passing pair is reported. Symmetric $\gamma$ need not imply
symmetric $\Gamma$: the rival comparisons differ. Net direction is not leak
direction.

\subsection{Permutation correction and flow labels}
Within each market, 999 symmetric permutations shuffle each firm's bids
independently within its own strata: $H$ at L1 and $(C_i,H)$ at L2. Define
\begin{equation}
g_{ij}=\hat\gamma_{ij}-\frac{1}{999}\sum_{r=1}^{999}\hat\gamma^{(r)}_{ij},
\qquad s_{ij}=\min\{\Gamma_{ij}[g],\Gamma_{ji}[g]\}.
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
% source: docs/PREREG.md section 5
Corrections remain signed. A candidate requires a permutation $p$-value for
raw influence at most $0.05/6$ and both corrected net directions positive;
only a unique candidate is reported. The test concerns influence, not net
influence. The raw fixed-threshold rule is reported separately.
The secondary leak score sums the two corrected leaks; a leak candidate
requires either direction to pass $0.05/12$, again with unique-pair reporting.
% source: docs/PREREG.md section 5

Flow labels are both directions detected; one detected, reverse weak;
neither detected, both weak; or unclear. ``Weak'' requires $p\ge0.20$ and a
corrected leak at most $0.05$ bits. Labels cover designated pairs and the full
detection pipeline. Weak evidence does not establish absence; one-way flow
can arise in a cartel with unilateral sharing.
% source: docs/PREREG.md section 7
Permutation validity requires within-stratum exchangeability, which adaptive
policies can violate. Per-market alarm rates are descriptive, without a
general calibration guarantee or error control across all analyses.

\subsection{Baselines and evaluation}
The L1 correlation baseline is uncorrected Cram\'er's V. L2 uses absolute
Pearson correlation after subtracting each firm's mean bid at its own cost
within the market; constant residuals score zero and signed correlations are
also retained. These baselines do not adjust for history. A strength-only
benchmark ranks the strong pair first, exposing a localisation shortcut.

The frozen Qwen3.5-9B judge uses thinking and returns raw pair probabilities
from rules and masked round records, with costs only at L2 and no treatment
information. There is no recalibration or answer selection; content failures
remain failures. Comparisons use common judged markets and report coverage;
descriptive Brier scores state the artificial prevalence. A Gemma-4-12B
counter-judge was attempted on core N0/C1-SS indices 0--9 at both levels. Qwen judging
Qwen remains a family-overlap limitation.
% source: docs/PREREG.md section 8

The market is the analysis unit. AUROC (area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve) is the probability that a randomly chosen treatment
market scores above a control market, with half credit for ties. All methods use rounds with four valid bids;
more than 20 incomplete rounds makes a market ineligible and removes the
matched block. A no-bid-category sensitivity retains missing bids. The sole
confirmatory test compares designated-pair L2 net scores in Qwen C1-SS and
N0 indices 0--19 over 200 rounds. With
99,999 within-block label swaps (seed 20261027),
\begin{equation}
p=\frac{1+\#\{\mathrm{AUC}^{*}\ge\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{100000};
\qquad \text{reject if }\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{obs}}>0.5\text{ and }p\le0.05.
\label{eq:primary}
\end{equation}
% source: docs/PREREG.md sections 4 and 11
This tests paired label exchangeability, not the composite null AUROC
$\le0.5$. Secondary estimates use 10,000 whole matched-block bootstraps and
pointwise 95\% percentile intervals. Top-1 is the fraction of credit for ranking the assigned pair first,
shared equally across ties. Localisation also reports reciprocal rank,
correct/wrong unique reports, and abstention; $1/6$ is a
guessing benchmark, not a null. Honest-market alarms include counts and
Clopper--Pearson intervals. E1 flow detection is not a collusion positive.
% source: docs/PREREG.md section 6

A2 tests paired fringe-placement swaps with Holm correction across seven
endpoints, not a pure model-sameness mechanism. Pooled fingerprints align
roles, bootstrap markets, and permute within markets. The exploratory probe
regenerates a partner's message under counterfactual cost, then samples bids
with the recipient's cost and history fixed; draws remain nested in markets
(Appendix~\ref{app:stats}).
% source: docs/PREREG.md sections 7 and 9, addendum A2

\subsection{Out-of-sample replication and judge-budget check}
\label{sec:addenda}
After the final analysis, two additions were written into the
pre-registration before their data were collected (Addenda v2.2 and v2.3,
Appendix~\ref{app:stats}). First, E1, C2 and C1-SS were collected again on
fresh market indices 20--39 with unchanged prompts, sampling, seeds and
200 rounds. Each new market shares its cost sequence with the already
collected honest N0 market of the same index, which serves as its matched
control. The frozen code analyses these markets as a separate cohort. The
pre-specified criteria are: for E1, the L2 net-minus-correlation interval
lies above zero; for C2, the joint-profit uplift over N0 lies above zero;
for C1-SS, the L2 net AUROC interval lies above $0.5$. Because the original
results were known, this is a replication of observed patterns, not a
blind test. Second, an exploratory check re-ran the Qwen judge with a
24,000-token instead of a 12,000-token output limit (L2; core N0, C1-SS and
C1-SF markets 0--19; everything else unchanged).
Re-analysing identical markets as a separate cohort reproduces correlation,
strength and paper scores exactly; net and leak scores change only by
permutation Monte Carlo noise (largest AUROC difference $0.0075$).
% source: docs/PREREG.md Addenda v2.2-v2.3 (check cohort note)

\subsection{Pre-registration and deviations}
Design and executable analysis were frozen before collection; A1--A3
preceded LLM outcome analysis. Seven deviations cover warm-up transport;
fenced-JSON parsing and labelled Gemma/Ministral recollection; caching,
streaming and checkpoints; disk exhaustion and relocation; later recovery,
call ceilings, loader paths and an exceeded budget cap; judge transport
recovery and budget cancellation of breadth judging; and the operations of
the two addenda. Core
Qwen prompts and sampling were retained. Appendix~\ref{app:stats} records
the freezes, amendment, and deviations separately.
